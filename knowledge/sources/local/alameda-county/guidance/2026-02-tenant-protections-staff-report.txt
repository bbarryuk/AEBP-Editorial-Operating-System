                       ADOPTED ORDINANCES

                       Just Cause
                       On February 4, 2025 (Item No. 44), your Board adopted the Just Cause for Eviction
                       Ordinance, which closely follows state law. This ordinance lays out a protective legal
                       framework to govern when and how a landlord may evict a tenant. Rental housing
                       providers may only evict tenants when they can show one of the enumerated “just
                       causes” is being used. These causes are divided between “At-Fault” reasons which
                       relate to violations of a lease or other unlawful action by a tenant and “No-Fault”
                       reasons such as owner move-in, substantial rehabilitation, and removal of a unit from
                       the rental market.

                       The ordinance does not include certain types of housing, including most single-family
                       homes. Single family homes that are owned by corporations are exempt, and just cause
                       protections are available to these tenants if they are aware that the property owner is a
                       corporation. However, since corporations are not required to disclose ownership to the
                       tenant or the county, each time a tenant receives an eviction notice, they must request
                       ownership information to learn if their property is covered. HCD is exploring ways to
                       easily access and provide this information to tenants. The ordinance also sets out
                       required relocation due to a tenant in the case of a no-fault eviction and requires
                       sending eviction notices to the County Community Development Agency (CDA).

                       Since March 2025 when the Ordinance took effect, HCD has received 153 notices,
                       mostly for nonpayment of rent.

HCD collects countywide unlawful detainer filing data quarterly from the Alameda County
Superior Court, which shows 62 unlawful detainers were filed for Unincorporated County
residents between March and November 2025. During this same period, HCD received 108
unlawful detainer notices.

HCD receives more eviction notices than are ultimately filed in court. This is because informal
evictions, those that never reach Court, are far more common than formal ones resulting from a
Court ruling. According to the U.S. Census Bureau’s American Housing Survey (AHS), informal
evictions occur 5.5 times more often than formal court evictions. In such cases a tenant “self-
evict” to avoid having an eviction on their record after receiving an eviction notice or other
communication from their landlord.

Using the AHS data, the County would have expected to receive approximately 300 eviction
notices between March and November 2025, given the 62 court filings.

While the County’s ability to enforce Just Cause protections on informal evictions is limited, the
low number of notices filed with HCD suggest there could be a larger number of rental units that
are not complying with the ordinance or are not subject to it. Fully capturing informal and formal
eviction trends in the unincorporated areas would require expansion of the ordinance to cover all
rental units and creation of a rental registry.

To ensure tenants know their rights under the Ordinance and rental housing providers follow Just
Cause protections, further education and outreach services are necessary. With American Rescue
Plan Act (ARPA) funding, HCD has provided resources to both East Bay Rental Housing
Association (EBRHA) and My Eden Voice (MEV) to ensure both rental housing providers and
tenants are aware of the Just Cause ordinance and its requirements. With the end of the ARPA
program, additional resources are needed to ensure wide outreach and engagement to both
tenants and rental property owners. For the current year, Measure W accrued funding was used
to augment contracts and continue services. Future year funds would require additional
investment.

Mediation
On November 12, 2024 (Item No. 33), your Board adopted the Rental Housing Dispute
Resolution Ordinance. This ordinance requires landlords and tenants to engage in good faith
mediation to resolve certain types of qualifying disputes upon the request of either party. The
Ordinance specifies that the County will cover the cost of the intake and facilitation for this
service.

On December 10, 2024 (Item No. 19), your Board approved a contract with the Center for
Community Dispute Settlement (CCDS) to provide mediation for rental housing providers and
tenants upon request, as required by the Rental Housing Dispute Resolution Ordinance. CCDS
has partnered with community groups and County departments to ensure tenants and rental
housing providers are referred to the mediation program.

PENDING ORDINANCES

Fair Chance
In December 2022, your Board passed the first reading of the Fair Chance Housing Ordinance,
aiming to reduce housing discrimination against individuals with criminal records. The ordinance
prohibited landlords from inquiring about or using an applicant's criminal history in housing
decisions, with exemptions for owner-occupied properties with four or fewer units and subleases.
As part of the Partnership for the Bay’s Future work, Fair Chance was discussed in nineteen
stakeholder meetings, during which it was identified as one of eight priority tenant protection
recommendations.

This policy built upon similar laws in Berkeley and Oakland. The legal landscape for such
ordinances was influenced by the Yim v. City of Seattle case. In 2023, the Ninth Circuit Court
upheld Seattle's prohibition on rental housing providers taking adverse actions based on criminal
history but struck down Seattle’s provision banning inquiries into such history, citing First
Amendment concerns. The case was in the courts for several years.

The 9th Circuit issued its final ruling on the Yim case in August of 2025; the final remaining
issue was whether the Seattle ordinance could survive if the provision banning inquiries was
severed. The Court determined it could.

County Counsel staff are drafting a revised Fair Chance ordinance that adheres to the Yim
decision and anticipates bringing the draft to your Committee for consideration this Summer
after meeting with stakeholders. Staff will be presenting about Fair Chance to your Board’s
Public Protection Committee on February 26, 2026.

Rental Registry
Local governments implement rental registration systems to enhance housing safety, improve
code enforcement, and gain better data on rental market trends. These systems allow
municipalities to track property ownership and contact information, ensure compliance with
health and safety standards, and protect tenants from substandard housing conditions.
Discussions regarding establishing a rental registry for units in unincorporated Alameda County
have been underway since 2020, driven by concerns over rising rents, eviction risks, and
substandard housing conditions in areas like Ashland, Cherryland, and San Lorenzo. With the
passage of AB1482 in 2019, annual allowable statewide rent increases are tied to the base rent
established in January 2020. However, without a registration system that includes information
about the base rent, it is difficult to monitor rent increases for AB1482 enforcement at a local
level. A Rental Registration program is a proactive measure to build the required infrastructure
to regulate and oversee rental properties in each jurisdiction.

Proactive Rental Inspection Pilot Program Ordinance
In September 2025, staff were directed to develop a 3.5-year proactive inspection pilot in
Ashland, Cherryland, and Hayward Acres. This pilot introduces regular rental inspections to
ensure safe, habitable housing conditions, targeting unincorporated areas with high renter
populations. It prevents habitability-related evictions and supports long-term housing quality.
This aligns with County goals of improving housing health and safety while preventing
displacement in under-resourced communities. This pilot program is anticipated to be brought to
your Committee this Summer after stakeholder meetings, and initial funding will be included in
CDA’s Measure W proposal for the Unincorporated County.


Landlord-Tenant Relations Ordinance
On June 24, 2025, your Board approved receiving a $400,000 grant from the Metropolitan
Transportation Commission for CDA work on transit-oriented community policies, a portion of
which will be used to fund stakeholder outreach and staff time for creation of a landlord-tenant
relations ordinance. This ordinance will include provisions against harassment of or retaliation
against tenants, as well as clarity regarding rights and responsibilities of both parties. The
ordinance will also include a forum for expressing landlord and tenant concerns. Stakeholder
engagement is expected to begin in Summer 2026.

Program Updates

Fair Housing – ECHO Contract
ECHO provides Fair Housing Counseling and Investigation, and Tenant/Landlord Counseling
and Dispute Resolution services to urban and Unincorporated Alameda County residents.
Services are targeted to eliminate discriminatory housing practices, provide information on basic
housing rights and responsibilities for landlords and tenants, and provide mediation or
conciliation to resolve disputes, specifically those related to residents and landlords negatively
affected by the COVID-19 pandemic. From October through December 2025, ECHO served a
total of 198 unduplicated clients. For Fair Housing, ECHO served 58 unduplicated clients. There
were a total of 38 inquiries and 20 investigations. Cases covered familial status, physical
disability, national origin, race, source of income, gender and sexual orientation. For
tenant/rental property owner services, ECHO served 140 clients, resulting in 199 services, with
the following primary concerns: evictions, deposits, harassment, retaliation, entry, habitability
and rent increases.

Alameda County Housing Secure
In 2018, your Board approved the creation of the Alameda County Housing Secure Program.
Through this program, the County funds a coalition of legal aid organizations to provide
counseling, know your rights training, and, when possible, representation to tenants who cannot
afford such services. This is a County-wide service, though ARPA funds were used to expand
resources for Unincorporated County tenants specifically. The cities of Alameda, Berkeley,
Emeryville, Hayward, Livermore, Oakland, and Pleasanton have similar contracts with Centro
Legal or another legal services provider to provide greater capacity to their residents over that
provided by AC Housing Secure. Currently, AC Housing Secure providers do not have the
funding or staffing capacity to provide full-scope legal service to every tenant who needs
assistance.

A Bay Area Housing Finance Report found that there are 261 eviction fillings annually for every
legal assistance attorney provided by AC Housing Secure and other similar programs—the
recommended sustainable number of cases per year is 40-50. AC Housing Secure has served
5,177 tenants and 391 homeowners since the launch of the program in 2018.

As part of a temporary pilot program supported by ARPA funds, the ACHS program included
independent in-court mediation services for landlord-tenant disputes, focusing on assisting low-
income tenants at risk of displacement. Between February 2024 and August 2025, 142
mediations were conducted and 99% of these evictions resulted in either a prevention of loss of
the current housing, a negotiated or facilitated move-out, or another housing benefit. The funding
for this pilot has now ended.

Housing Provider Resource Center
On July 9, 2024, your Board approved a contract with EBRHA for creation and operation of the
Housing Provider Resource Center, using ARPA funds. The Center provides training, referrals,
and consultation services for rental housing providers in the unincorporated County. Since its
launch, the Resource Center has fielded almost 300 inquiries and hosted over 70 events.

This Spring, HCD will contract with EBRHA for $150,000 in Measure W funds to continue their
work with the Housing Providers Resource Center as well as $200,000 to conduct a minor home
repair program for repairs costing up to $10,000 in rental units where low-income tenants reside.

My Eden Voice
On July 26, 2022, the Board approved a contract with MEV, a community-based organizing and
advocacy group, to undertake outreach, education, trainings, and organization of Unincorporated
County residents, using federal American Rescue Plan Act (ARPA) funds. During phase one,
MEV completed a training initiative for residents, leaders, and grassroots organizations and
administered the Leadership Academy for 54 participants and 4 organizations. Phase 2 of the
program is ongoing and includes training, workshops, and development of civic participation
models for unincorporated county residents.
