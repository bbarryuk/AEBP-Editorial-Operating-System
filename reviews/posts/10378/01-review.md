## GATE-LEGAL-ACCURACY

### 10378-F01

> eviction papers need three real delivery attempts and GPS-stamped photo proof of service

The summaries conflate personal and alternative service and omit the court order and photo exceptions. Align Quick Answer, table, FAQ, excerpts and both scripts. CCP §§415.20, 415.45, 417.10, 1166. Source: https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB747/2025 and https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB2042/2025

**Replacement**

Before substituted service or court-authorized posting of the summons and complaint, the server must try personal delivery in good faith at least three times on three different days at three different times. For personal service, substituted service or posting, proof generally includes a dated, timed and location-stamped photo for each attempt or completed service. If location signal is unavailable, document the missing stamp; if taking a photo would compromise safety, document the service or attempt and explain why. For a home or business, photograph the door if reasonably accessible; otherwise photograph the entrance and explain why the door was inaccessible. The eviction complaint already had to state the method of serving the termination notice; starting January 1 it must also state the date, time and location.

### 10378-F02

> a rental ad with a virtually staged or otherwise altered photo must say so and show the original

The short copy and FAQ contradict the full section's exception. Routine edits are excluded only when they preserve the property's representation. Civil Code §1940.11(a)–(b). Source: https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB2025/id/3460442

**Replacement**

Disclose digitally altered images next to or on the image. If an altered image came from a photograph, include the original or tell viewers where to find it through a public link, URL or QR code. Fully AI-generated images need disclosure even when there is no original photograph. Routine edits such as cropping or brightness adjustments are excluded when they preserve how the property is represented.

### 10378-F03

> During a declared emergency, rent increases over ten percent are price gouging.

The script states the threshold without the statutory baseline, defenses, or authorized extensions. The article's repair example omits the rental status or documented rental intent condition. Penal Code §396(e), (g), (j)(11). Source: https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB1365/2025

**Replacement**

During a covered emergency price-gouging period, the law generally bars advertised, offered or charged rent more than 10% above the statutory rental-price baseline, regardless of lease length. The baseline depends on the property's rental history and other facts. The law provides defenses for a pre-emergency increase agreed to by the tenant and qualifying repairs or additions with the required rental history or documented rental intent. Lower local limits still apply.

### 10378-F04

> linked in online listings and handed out with every application

The shorthand and checklist imply every third-party listing must carry a link. The detailed article already recognizes the limits. Civil Code §1942.7.5(b), (c), (g), (h). Source: https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB1296/2025

**Replacement**

Starting April 1, provide electronic access on the property website if it has one and in digital ads you control. Include access in third-party listings and feeds where the platform permits or supports it. Give applicants the written policy with each application and include a space to acknowledge receipt.

### 10378-F05

> No requirement to state when, where and how the notice was served

Prior CCP §1166 already requires the method. The new statute adds three fields. This also affects the body and FAQ. Source: https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB747/2025

**Replacement**

Through 2026: State how the termination notice was served. From January 1, 2027: State the method and add the date, time and location of service.

### 10378-F06

> A lawful eviction for another reason, or one that began before the declaration, can continue.

As drafted, this can read as a carveout allowing above-cap re-rental after any otherwise lawful eviction. Penal Code §396(f), (m). Source: https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB1365/2025

**Replacement**

The law allows evictions for lawful reasons. Separately, it bars evicting during the protected period and then renting or offering the unit above what the evicted tenant could be charged. An eviction lawfully begun before the declaration may continue.
