# Emeryville Residential Landlord and Tenant Relations Ordinance

Existed for one reason: post 9008 ("East Bay Rent Control Comparison," Week 1 cornerstone) originally sourced Emeryville's landlord-tenant rules to a third-party law-firm summary (Bornstein Law) despite the City of Emeryville publishing its own primary-source materials on the same requirements. A ChatGPT pre-publish audit flagged this as a sourcing-hierarchy gap — the repo's evidence standard prefers local ordinance/government sources over advocacy or law-firm summaries, and Emeryville has a clean primary source available. This entry gives future drafts (especially the Week 4 Emeryville deep dive) a first-party citation instead of defaulting to a secondary summary.

## What's actually true

Emeryville has no rent-control/AGA ordinance — AB 1482 governs the rate directly for units it covers. But Emeryville's own **Residential Landlord and Tenant Relations Ordinance** imposes real requirements independent of any rent cap:

- **Valid business license required.** A landlord must hold a valid residential landlord business license (per Emeryville Municipal Code Title 3, Ch. 1) to legally terminate a tenancy — no license, no valid termination, regardless of the cause.
- **Tenant rights notice required.** Landlords must give tenants the ordinance's required rights notice before proceeding with termination.
- **City Clerk filing, 10-day window.** A true and accurate copy of the termination notice must be delivered to the City Clerk within 10 calendar days.
- **Qualifying cause required.** Termination must be either "for cause" (nonpayment, lease violation, illegal activity) or "no fault" (owner move-in, unit demolition, etc.) — matches the general just-cause structure seen elsewhere in the East Bay, but Emeryville's own ordinance defines its own specific grounds (including unusual ones like a landlord returning from sabbatical or military deployment — verify the current grounds list against the ordinance itself before citing a specific ground, this entry doesn't reproduce the full list).
- **Relocation assistance for no-fault terminations, scaled by landlord size:**
  - Small landlords (4 units or fewer): greater of one month's HUD fair market rent (FMR) or one month's current rent.
  - Large landlords (5+ units): greater of five months' FMR or four times current monthly rent.
  - FMR figures are HUD's Oakland-Fremont area figures and update on HUD's own schedule — re-check the current year's FMR table before citing a dollar figure, not just the multiplier.

## Week 4 deep-dive expansion (verified 2026-09-19, post 9215)

**Source-currency warning:** the ECHO Fair Housing PDF (current only through Ord. 17-007) is STALE. Chapter 5-40 took effect Apr 1, 2017 (Ord. 16-011) and was amended by Ord. 20-006 (eff. May 21, 2020; amended §§5-40.02, 5-40.03(e)(1)(i)) and Ord. 20-009 (eff. June 4, 2020; amended §5-40.02, repealed §5-40.04(e)), plus COVID-era changes. Cite eCode360, not ECHO.

**Definitions (§5-40.02):** small landlord = 4 or fewer covered units, or a landlord of a group residential facility of any size; large landlord = more than 4 covered units in Emeryville. Covered units exclude short-stay hotels/motels, owner-occupied residences with 2 or fewer lodgers, nonprofit co-ops, rent-controlled units, certain care facilities, government-owned housing.

**Preconditions (§5-40.03(a)-(d)):** valid residential landlord business license; notice of tenant rights given; termination notice in the form required by §5-40.08; landlord has not accepted and will not accept rent or other consideration.

**For-cause grounds (§5-40.03(e)(1), ten):** (i) nonpayment after 3-day written notice, with a repayment-plan offer of at least 12 months required for affected tenants (COVID-era amendment); (ii) material breach; (iii) illegal use; (iv) dangerous/unsanitary condition; (v) refusal of access (Civ. Code §1954); (vi) refusal to sign substantially similar lease extension; (vii) occupancy above 2 persons per bedroom plus 1; (viii) landlord sabbatical return; (ix) military deployment return; (x) condominium conversion with relocation.

**No-fault grounds (§5-40.03(e)(2), four):** (i) demolition/permanent removal from rental use; (ii) landlord or landlord's parent/child moves in as permanent residence at least 10 months per year for at least 2 years; (iii) temporary uninhabitability for repairs; (iv) capital improvements with permits in hand.

**Relocation (§5-40.04):** required for no-fault terminations unless caused by natural disaster-type events. Small: greater of 1x HUD FMR or 1x current rent. Large: greater of 5x HUD FMR or 4x current monthly rent. Divided equally among occupants; delivered by final tenancy date. (§5-40.04(e) repealed by Ord. 20-009.)

**Right to return (§5-40.02(j)):** same unit offered if returned to market within 2 years, tenant supplies mailing address and accepts within 30 days of written offer; survives change of ownership.

**City Clerk (10 days):** copy of termination notice plus executed rental agreement to City Clerk, 1333 Park Avenue.

**Remedies (§5-40.06):** (a) civil action for damages/injunction by tenants or City; (b) failure of any precondition invalidates notice (City-filing failure excepted) with attorney fees to prevailing tenant; (c) tenants may challenge coverage.

**Business license timing (Municipal Code Ch. 3-1):** certificates issued Jan 1, expire Dec 31 (§3-1.107(a)); delinquent if unpaid by Mar 1 (§3-1.116(a)(1)); penalty 5%/month up to 25% (§3-1.116); interest 1.5%/month (§3-1.119). Residential rate 0.10% of prior-year gross receipts with $25 minimum: from City FAQ (updated 2025-07-31) ONLY — Ch. 3-1 states only a commercial-rental rate (0.35%, §3-1.128); do not present as code text.

**AB 1482 exemption citations:** §1947.12(d)(1) deed-restricted affordable; (d)(4) certificate of occupancy within previous 15 years; (d)(5) single-unit owner-natural-person exemption requiring the statutory written notice; (a)(2) two increments in 12 months.

**Framing support:** City's Tenant Protection page says its local just-cause ordinance predates Sept 1, 2019, so it governs terminations while other AB 1482 provisions still apply. As of 2026-09-19 the page still showed the 6.3% cap (Aug 2025-Jul 2026).
