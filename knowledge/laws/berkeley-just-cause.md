# Berkeley Just Cause for Eviction — Waiting Period Correction

Existed for one reason: the Berkeley PM page (post 2346) and a draft of the Sept Week 1 cornerstone both stated that just-cause eviction protection "applies after 12 months of continuous occupancy, under Measure MM." That claim does not hold up against the ordinance text and has been corrected in both places (2026-08-29).

## What's actually true

**BMC 13.76.130 ("Just cause required for eviction") has no blanket occupancy minimum before just-cause protection applies.** Confirmed by direct fetch of the ordinance text. A handful of *specific enhanced protections* within the same section carry their own residency thresholds — e.g., additional relocation assistance where a household member has resided one year or more, and stronger protections for families with minor children (12 months) or seniors/disabled tenants/large-portfolio tenants (5 years) — but these are add-on safeguards layered on top of baseline just cause, not a condition on whether just cause applies at all.

**Measure MM (passed November 2020, not 2018)** is about registration requirements for partially-covered units (post-1980 construction, most single-family homes, most condos, certain subsidized units) and reclassifying pandemic-era back rent as consumer debt. It does not impose a 12-month just-cause waiting period. The "12 months under Measure MM" claim was most likely a conflation with **AB 1482's statewide 12-month just-cause rule**, which would only be relevant for a Berkeley unit not covered by the local ordinance at all — a narrow edge case, not the general rule.

## Bottom line for future drafts

Berkeley's local just-cause ordinance applies from day one of a covered tenancy, the same as Oakland's and Richmond's local ordinances (see `rent-caps.md` and `richmond-rent-program.md` for those). Don't attribute a 12-month wait to Berkeley's local ordinance or to Measure MM specifically. If a specific unit's coverage category is in question (fully vs. partially covered), that's a coverage-rules question, not a timing question — and coverage is where Berkeley's real nuance lives.
